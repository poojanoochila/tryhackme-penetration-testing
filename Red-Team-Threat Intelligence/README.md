# Red Team Threat Intelligence

> **Platform:** TryHackMe  
> **Difficulty:** Easy  
> **Category:** Red Team / Threat Intelligence / Adversary Emulation

## Overview

This repository documents my learning experience from the **Red Team Threat Intelligence** room on TryHackMe. The room explores how Cyber Threat Intelligence (CTI) supports Red Team operations by enabling adversary emulation based on real-world attacker behaviors.

Rather than simply performing offensive actions, Red Teams use threat intelligence to study known threat actors, understand their Tactics, Techniques, and Procedures (TTPs), and replicate their behavior during controlled security assessments. The room also introduces threat intelligence frameworks such as MITRE ATT&CK and TIBER-EU, along with techniques for planning intelligence-driven campaigns.

---

- Understand the role of Cyber Threat Intelligence (CTI) in Red Team operations.
- Learn how TTPs support adversary emulation.
- Explore the MITRE ATT&CK framework.

- Explore intelligence-driven campaign development.

## Learning Methodology

Throughout this room, I approached each task from the perspective of a Red Team operator planning an engagement against a target organization.

My learning process included:

- Understanding how threat intelligence is collected and analyzed.
- Learning how Red Teams consume CTI to emulate real-world attackers.
- Exploring threat intelligence frameworks used during engagement planning.
- Studying MITRE ATT&CK techniques associated with known threat groups.
- Learning how adversary behaviors are mapped across the Cyber Kill Chain.
- Understanding how CTI can influence C2 traffic, tooling, and operational behaviors.
- Building a threat intelligence-driven campaign using publicly available intelligence.

This methodology demonstrated how threat intelligence transforms offensive assessments into realistic adversary simulations.

## Key Takeaways

One of the biggest lessons from this room was understanding that **effective Red Team engagements begin with intelligence rather than exploitation**. By studying real threat actors and their behaviors, Red Teams can emulate realistic attack scenarios that better evaluate an organization's detection and response capabilities.

I also learned that frameworks such as **MITRE ATT&CK** and **TIBER-EU** provide structured approaches for organizing threat intelligence, mapping attacker techniques, and developing realistic adversary emulation campaigns.
