# Red Team Fundamentals

> **Platform:** TryHackMe  
> **Difficulty:** Easy  
> **Category:** Red Team / Adversary Emulation / Cybersecurity Fundamentals

## Overview

This repository documents my learning experience from the **Red Team Fundamentals** room on TryHackMe. The room introduces the principles of red teaming, explaining how red team engagements differ from traditional penetration testing and vulnerability assessments.

The room also covers the planning, execution, and documentation of red team operations, highlighting how organizations simulate real-world adversaries to evaluate their security posture, detection capabilities, and incident response processes.

---

## Objectives

- Understand the purpose of Red Team engagements.
- Differentiate Red Teaming from Vulnerability Assessments and Penetration Testing.
- Learn the roles and responsibilities within a Red Team engagement.
- Understand the Cyber Kill Chain.
- Learn how Rules of Engagement (RoE) define engagement boundaries.
- Explore the planning and documentation involved in adversary emulation.

---

## Learning Methodology

During this room, I approached each task from the perspective of a Red Team operator participating in a professional engagement.

My learning process included:

- Understanding the goals and objectives of adversary emulation.
- Comparing Red Team exercises with vulnerability assessments and penetration tests.
- Learning how engagement scope and objectives are established.
- Reviewing Rules of Engagement to understand operational boundaries.
- Exploring the responsibilities of different teams involved in an engagement.
- Understanding how attackers progress through each phase of the Cyber Kill Chain.
- Reviewing operational planning documents used during Red Team assessments.

This approach provided insight into how structured planning ensures realistic yet controlled security assessments.

Throughout this room, I learned about several core concepts that are essential for professional Red Team operations:

- The difference between vulnerability assessments, penetration tests, and Red Team engagements.
- The importance of clearly defining engagement scope and objectives.
- How Rules of Engagement ensure safe and authorized testing.
- The responsibilities of Red, Blue, and White Teams during an engagement.

- Why adversary emulation focuses on evaluating an organization's detection and response capabilities rather than simply identifying vulnerabilities.

---

## Key Takeaways

One of the most valuable lessons from this room was understanding that **Red Teaming is designed to emulate real-world attackers rather than simply exploit vulnerabilities**. Unlike traditional security assessments, Red Team engagements evaluate people, processes, and technology together to measure an organization's ability to detect, respond to, and recover from sophisticated attacks.

The room also highlighted the importance of structured planning, clear communication, and well-defined Rules of Engagement to ensure that adversary simulations remain effective, controlled, and aligned with organizational objectives.
